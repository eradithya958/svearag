# Taxes for foreign workers in Sweden (SINK vs Ordinary Income Tax)

If you live abroad and work in Sweden for a period shorter than six months, or if you live in Sweden temporarily, your tax liability is calculated under specific Swedish tax legislation.

## 1. Special Income Tax for Non-Residents (SINK)
SINK (*Särskild inkomstskatt för utomlands bosatta*) is a flat tax rate of **25 percent** levied directly at source on Swedish earned income.
- **Key Characteristics:**
  - Flat rate of 25% with no deductions permitted (e.g. no travel deductions, no basic tax-free allowance /*grundavdrag*/).
  - You do not need to file an annual Swedish income tax return (*inkomstdeklaration*) for income taxed under SINK.
  - SINK must be formally applied for through Skatteverket by either the employee or employer.

## 2. Ordinary Municipal and State Income Tax (A-skatt)
If you work in Sweden for **more than six months**, you are considered regularly tax resident in Sweden.
- You will pay standard municipal income tax (*kommunalskatt*), which averages approximately **32 percent** depending on the municipality (*kommun*).
- You are entitled to the basic Swedish personal deduction (*grundavdrag*).
- If your annual taxable income exceeds the state tax threshold (*skiktgräns*, approximately **SEK 598,500**), a **20 percent state income tax** applies to the portion of income above this threshold.
- You must file an annual Swedish tax return by early May each calendar year.

## Tax Registration (A-skattesedel)
All individuals working in Sweden must obtain a preliminary tax assessment notice (*A-skattsedel*) or tax card from Skatteverket so that their employer can withhold the accurate tax rate.
